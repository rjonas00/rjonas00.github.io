About

I'm a Master's student in Astronomy at the University of Vienna, working on the connection between galaxies and their dark matter halos using the TNG300 cosmological simulation. I'm drawn to large datasets — from simulations and observations alike — and to the computational and statistical methods needed to turn them into physical insight. My aim is a research career in cosmology, contributing to the international, collaborative effort to understand how the structure of the universe formed and evolved.

Alongside this, I've worked hands-on with radio and optical telescopes — measuring 21 cm Doppler shifts in the Milky Way, analyzing transits, and mapping the interstellar medium with 2MASS and GAIA catalogs. I like combining these different vantage points — observations, catalogs, and simulations — and comparing what each one tells us.

Research

I am a research project member in the Data Science in Astrophysics & Cosmology group of Prof. Hahn at the University of Vienna, where I work with the TNG300 simulation to study the Lagrangian bias function of galaxies. This involves examining galaxies of different stellar masses — with a particular focus on star-forming and satellite galaxies — and investigating how assembly history shapes their properties.

Through this work I've learned to handle terabyte-scale data efficiently, optimize memory usage, and write high-performance code, as well as run simulations and analyses on supercomputing clusters using Slurm.
